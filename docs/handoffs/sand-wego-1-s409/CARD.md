## Build the loop

1. **Choose a mode before the run.** Extend the existing Hormuz briefing with a compact CONTINUOUS / WEGO choice and an honest explanation: “WeGo vs AI. Give orders, resolve together, review.” Continuous stays the default. Mode is locked once the run starts; changing it means explicitly restarting, not converting a live battle. A new run starts held, with GOD off. WeGo uses a five-minute simulated turn by default, with 1 / 5 / 15 minute choices before starting. Store the selected duration as validated session configuration, not a global. The snapshot and legacy routes remain continuous and say so rather than displaying an inert mode control.
2. **Plan without advancing the world.** Session-owned phases are PLANNING → RESOLVING → REVIEW, plus the existing terminal result. Planning allows the existing own-unit waypoint, throttle, radar and ROE controls. Stage manual ENGAGE intents visibly with a cancel action. No missile, ammunition debit, cooldown or combat-log event occurs merely from queuing an order. Check immediate validity against current known contacts, then revalidate on execution. Reject stale, illegal or unaffordable intents once with a plain reason. Bound duplicate/double-click requests so one intent cannot spend ammunition twice.
3. **Commit both sides from the held world.** COMMIT ORDERS seals the player's draft and calls the existing AI planner once from its own side's contact store at that same turn-start state. Do not reveal OPFOR orders. During resolution suppress repeated AI *planning*, not its mission/doctrine context: keep waypoint consumption, movement, sensors and contact aging, automatic ROE fire, missile defense, impacts, scripted events and the existing post-combat terminal pass. Route chase, CAP retarget, radar and withdrawal decisions wait until the next planning boundary. Authored TCA effects still execute as scenario rules. Do not pass `undefined` for all AI context merely to disable replanning, and do not remove contact-gated AI/combat protections.
4. **Resolve one exact interval.** Advance through the existing simulation phases with bounded, fixed simulated substeps to the committed `start + turnLength`, never beyond it. A terminal result stops earlier on its real authoritative tick, with same-tick carrier destruction still defeating transit success. Turn count increments once per committed turn. Manual fire intents execute once at the start of the burst before ordinary combat advances, with clear refusal feedback if invalid. Both sides then evolve together. Display the remaining simulated interval. Presentation speed may change wall-clock playback time, not rules, phase cadence or the number of simulated seconds. A slow/background frame must neither overshoot nor create a second turn. Keep the UI responsive.
5. **Enforce the lock in the session.** During RESOLVING and REVIEW, reject tactical commands through buttons, map gestures, hotkeys and direct command methods. Selection, panning, briefing, logs and platform inspection still work. Pausing resolution may hold presentation, but cannot reopen planning; RESUME finishes the same committed interval. Continuous autopause preferences are preserved but do not interrupt WeGo into editable planning: collect alerts for review instead. Do not leave an old HOLD cue under the phase or result. Double COMMIT, double NEXT TURN, repeated Space/Enter and rapid clicks must not create extra resolution or bypass a lock.
6. **Review then plan again.** At the boundary, hold time and show turn number, elapsed interval, own losses/damage and the already fog-filtered contacts/messages/combat record for that turn. No unobserved enemy names, positions, health, orders or omniscient kill list. NEXT TURN opens planning at the exact held state without advancing time. If the scenario ended, show its existing authoritative debrief instead; no NEXT TURN. PLAY AGAIN/RESET starts the selected mode afresh, clears turn phase/intents/counters/transient selection and targeting, and retains only documented preferences. No local-file save or cross-refresh recovery.

Fence: change session/engine command scheduling, React bindings and the existing terminal controls only as required, plus focused tests, help/status text and receipts. Keep platform values, weapons, damage rules, scenario units/events/objectives, facility/OSINT data, fonts, display modes and continuous-game behavior unchanged. No general engine rewrite, PRNG replacement, new AI strategy, networking, deployment or scope borrowed from SAND-SAVE-1.
